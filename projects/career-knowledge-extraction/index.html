AI workflow case study

Knowledge Extraction

A human-governed AI workflow for turning long-form career conversations into structured, traceable knowledge.

I turned one exported chat into a knowledge archive without reducing the original work to a single summary.

See an exampleExplore the workflow →

The operating question

How do you preserve meaning in a long, evolving discussion without flattening its history?

Start with the source.
Extract before distilling.
Keep human judgment at every consequential gate.

01 / Problem

Important career knowledge was buried in conversation history.

Long-running conversations can hold far more than final answers. They also contain the reasoning behind decisions, alternatives that were rejected, methods that changed over time, and questions that were never fully resolved.

Reconstructing the conversation from memory creates a different risk: treating recollection as evidence. A conventional summary can make the material easier to read while diluting the very context needed to use it responsibly.

The goal wasn't to make the conversation shorter. 

It was to make its durable knowledge findable, traceable, and usable.

02 / Approach

The source comes before conclusions.

I treated the conversation as an extraction problem before treating it as a writing problem.

Source before memory

The supplied export, not chat memory or unrelated documents, governed what could be extracted.

Coverage before compression

A lightweight map and focused extraction made it less likely that important reasoning would be lost before the material was distilled.

Extraction before synthesis

I organized and extracted the material before synthesizing it, so later conclusions rested on recovered material rather than an early impression.

03 / Operating model

Human judgment stayed in the loop.

I directed the workflow and made the final decision about how its output could be used.

Owner

Defined the problem and shaped the method.

  • Defined the task as extraction, not summary.
  • Set the source before extraction began.
  • Defined what the source could support.
  • Required extraction before synthesis.
  • Reviewed and approved the final synthesis.

AI agent

Analyzed the source and drafted outputs.

  • Proposed the workflow structure.
  • Mapped coverage across the source.
  • Extracted material by knowledge class.
  • Produced extraction artifacts and a draft synthesis.
  • Flagged limitations and unresolved gaps.

04 / Workflow

From source export to reusable knowledge.

The workflow created a deliberate chain from a verified source to an approved synthesis.

  1. 01

    Verify and preserve

    Establish the supplied transcript's identity and metadata before processing it.

  2. 02

    Build a coverage map

    Create a lightweight outline so the source can be navigated and reviewed as a whole.

  3. 03

    Extract by knowledge class

    Use focused passes to separate decisions, methodology, prompts, and philosophy or lessons.

  4. 04

    Synthesize after extraction

    Consolidate only after the coverage-oriented passes are complete and their limitations are visible.

Each stage required my approval, from the source through later reuse.

05 / Practical use

The conversation stays useful after it ends.

Together, the outputs preserved decisions and reasoning for later work without needing to reread the full conversation.

07 / Value

The next question did not start from scratch.

Later, I asked an agent to check the extracted material for gaps in my interview preparation. It drew on different reviewers’ perspectives to identify what to build next and what still needed development.

Instead of starting over, I could build the next stage from what had already been recovered.

08 / Evidence and limits

Evidence and limits belong together.

The project demonstrates a completed workflow cycle. 

It does not establish full automation, independent validation, or a universal method.

Demonstrated in this project

  • One conversation export was processed.
  • I set the direction while AI drafted outputs.
  • Five documents captured the source; synthesis brought them together.
  • I reviewed and approved the final synthesis.
  • The resulting archive supported later work.

Not established

  • How completely the process captured the source.
  • Repeatability across materially different sources.
  • Independent validation of the method.
  • A replacement for human judgment.
  • External adoption or deployment.
!

I applied this method to one web-based ChatGPT conversation, not my complete chat history.

Exporting the conversation was only the first step. Preserving and using it well was the challenge.
